The problem

On a phone call, the wrong language costs someone a fare, a delivery, or their dignity. India changes language every few hundred kilometres, its cities are full of people from other states, and everyone is proud of their own.

Bengaluru · ಕನ್ನಡ ⇄ हिन्दी

The driver who never needed Hindi.

Fewer than half of Bengaluru's people speak Kannada; its passengers come from every state. An auto driver who grew up speaking Kannada can't follow "gate number 2 ke paas", and can't answer back in Hindi. Both talk louder, the pickup is missed, the ride is cancelled, and too often the call turns into an argument on video.

The South First · Deccan Herald · Free Press Journal · The Week

Mumbai · हिन्दी ⇄ मराठी

The delivery partner who can't speak Marathi.

Many of the riders delivering food and parcels in Mumbai and Pune come from other states and speak Hindi. Customers expect Marathi. In 2025 a pizza delivery agent was told "Speak Marathi or we won't pay", and the video went viral. He was there to hand over a pizza.

Deccan Chronicle · Free Press Journal

Where Convox fits

Convox sits in the middle of the call. The driver speaks Kannada and the passenger hears Hindi; the delivery partner speaks Hindi and the customer hears Marathi. Nobody has to switch languages, and nobody gets mocked for the one they speak.

A rider or delivery partner takes dozens of these calls a day, from strangers. When language breaks one:

  • The parcelThe delivery agent can't find the address or get the OTP. The delivery fails, and a parcel sent back costs the courier ₹150–250.1
  • The 10-minute orderThe rider is at the wrong gate with the clock running. The order arrives late or gets cancelled.
  • The food order"Which tower? Which floor?" The food goes cold while the rider and customer guess at each other.
  • The bike-taxi pickup"Where are you?" in Kannada, "near the metro" in Hindi. The ride is cancelled and both sides lose.

How it works

An interpreter that joins the call and stays out of the way.

  1. 01

    Listens

    Knows when you've finished a thought. A "haan" or "hmm" doesn't interrupt.

  2. 02

    Understands

    Sarvam speech recognition in six Indian languages, detected from your first two seconds.

  3. 03

    Translates

    Sarvam Mayura translation. Every number is checked, so 47296 stays 47296.

  4. 04

    Speaks

    A natural Bulbul voice in the listener's language, after a soft chime.

Why it feels like a real call

Built for how India talks on the phone.

One voice at a time

A floor controller decides who has the line, so nobody hears two voices at once. Speak up to cut in.

Numbers come out right

OTPs, amounts, flat and phone numbers are checked digit by digit between what was said and what is heard.

Knows your language

Detected automatically. If it can't tell, one keypad press picks it.

No app, any phone

Works on a normal phone call through Exotel. For demos, two phones scan a QR code.

Keypad control

1 replays the last line. 0 turns interpreting off.

Private by default

No recording in production. Transcripts only with consent, in line with the DPDP Act.

How platforms use it

Inside the platform's calls, not another app.

01

Why not an app

iPhone apps can't hear phone calls at all, and Android locks call audio down further every release. Interpreting also needs both voices, and an app on one phone only has one.

02

Where it runs

Number masking already puts the platform's CPaaS, such as Exotel, in the middle of every rider–customer call, where both voices are. Convox joins there with a stream on each leg. Nothing changes on either phone or in the app.

03

When it switches on

Only when the rider's and the customer's languages differ (the app already knows from their profiles), when a rider taps a language button, or after a call that didn't connect them. Every other call is untouched.
