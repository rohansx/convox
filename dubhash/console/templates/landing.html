The problem

India talks in 22 scheduled languages.
Most phone calls assume one.

Over 45 crore people in India live away from where they were born (Census 2011). They deliver our parcels, drive our cabs, fix our pipes and call our clinics, often in a language that isn't theirs. When the two sides of a call don't share one, the call quietly fails.

01

The parcel goes back

A rider from Bihar is lost in a Pune housing society. The customer explains the way in Marathi; he understands half. Ten minutes later the order is marked "customer unreachable".

02

The symptom gets lost

A Gujarati-speaking grandmother on a teleconsult with an English-speaking doctor. A grandson translates what he can. "Twice a day after food" arrives as "twice a day".

03

One digit, wrong

OTPs, flat numbers, rupee amounts. Misheard across a language gap, a single wrong digit sends everyone back to the start of the call.

Today's workarounds: a relative on speakerphone, a supervisor on a three-way call, or hanging up.

The solution

An interpreter that joins the call,
and gets out of the way.

dubhash sits between the two phones. Nobody installs anything or presses a button: they talk the way they always do, and dubhash makes sure each of them hears the other in their own language.

  1. ◉

    Listens

    Knows when you've finished a thought, not just when you've gone quiet. A "haan" or "hmm" never interrupts.

  2. ✎

    Understands

    Indian speech recognition by Sarvam, in six languages. It works out which language you're speaking in the first two seconds.

  3. ⇄

    Translates

    Every number is read back and checked against the translation, so OTP 47296 stays 47296.

  4. ♪

    Speaks

    A natural Indian voice in the listener's language, after a soft chime, so they know it's the interpreter.

  5. ⏵

    Takes turns

    One voice at a time, like a real interpreter. Talk over it to cut in; press 1 on the keypad to hear the last line again.

Why it feels like a real call

Built for the way India actually talks on the phone.

One voice at a time

A floor controller decides who has the line, so you never hear two voices at once. Short back-channels are ignored; speak up to interrupt.

Numbers come out right

OTPs, amounts, flat and phone numbers are checked digit by digit between what you said and what the other person hears.

Knows your language

Detected automatically from your first words. If it can't tell, the keypad menu lets you choose in one press.

No app, any phone

Works on a normal phone call through Exotel, even from a ₹1,200 feature phone. For stage demos, two phones just scan a QR code.

Keypad control

1 replays the last translation. 0 turns interpreting off when both people turn out to share a language.

Private by default

Calls are not recorded in production. Transcripts are kept only with consent, in line with India's DPDP Act.
